How high is the world?

A map of height above sea level for the whole planet, sharp from the scale of continents down to single valleys, with the world's peaks and cities coloured by how high they stand. How much of the land is mountain, which countries sit highest, and how high do people really live?

Dr Tristan Hotham · Independent researcher · 2026 · Data: AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen; SRTM, GMTED2010, ETOPO1 and others); Wikidata; Natural Earth; English Wikipedia

    Terrain: AWS Terrain Tiles, Terrarium format (Mapzen, hosted on the Registry of Open Data on AWS). They combine, among others: SRTM (NASA and USGS, public domain); GMTED2010 (USGS and NGA, public domain); ETOPO1 (NOAA, Amante and Eakins 2009, public domain); NED and 3DEP (USGS, public domain); Canadian Digital Elevation Data (Natural Resources Canada, Open Government Licence Canada); EU-DEM (produced using Copernicus data and information funded by the European Union); Digital Elevation Model over Europe; the Australian 5 m DEM (Geoscience Australia, CC BY 4.0); New Zealand's 8 m DEM (LINZ, CC BY 4.0); Mexico's INEGI continental relief; Great Britain's OS Terrain 50 (Ordnance Survey, Open Government Licence, © Crown copyright and database right); ArcticDEM (Polar Geospatial Center, NSF). Full list and terms: github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md. Antarctica south of 85° S: ETOPO10, a 10 arc minute grid derived from ETOPO1. Peaks and cities: Wikidata (CC0). Peak supplements: Natural Earth elevation points (public domain) and English Wikipedia, 'List of mountain peaks by prominence' (CC BY-SA 4.0). Countries, lakes and places: Natural Earth (public domain). Base map and satellite tiles © Esri. Map library: Leaflet.
